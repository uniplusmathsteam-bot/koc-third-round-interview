Rate & RatioPre S2 Maths

Rates link amounts to time. Ratios compare like with like — and the colon “:” simply means divide.

What is a rate?

A rate compares two quantities with different units — often something that happens over time.

1. Rate = “how fast”

If you eat 3 buns in 1 minute, your eating rate is 3\ \text{buns}/\text{min}. Rate always ties an amount to time (or another different unit).

2. Travel rates

In this lesson, rate usually means how far you move in a given time — walking, cycling, driving…

\mathrm{speed}=\dfrac{\mathrm{distance}}{\mathrm{time}}

3. Units look like fractions

Write the distance unit over the time unit with a slash — just like a fraction bar:

\mathrm{km/hr}\quad\cdot\quad\mathrm{m/s}

4. Convert time first

1 minute = 60 seconds → 3\ \text{buns}/\text{min}=\dfrac{1}{20}\ \text{bun}/\text{s}.
1 hour = 60 minutes → 3\ \text{buns}/\text{min}=180\ \text{buns}/\text{hr}.

1\ \mathrm{km}=1000\ \mathrm{m} and 1\ \mathrm{hr}=3600\ \mathrm{s}, so:

\div 3.6\quad\text{or}\quad\times 3.6

\mathrm{km/hr}\to\mathrm{m/s}:\ \div 3.6
\mathrm{m/s}\to\mathrm{km/hr}:\ \times 3.6

What is a ratio?

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind (same units). For Hong Kong F2, remember: the colon is just division.

1. “:” means ÷

The ratio symbol is division written in another outfit:

a:b=\dfrac{a}{b}

Examples:

2:3=\dfrac{2}{3}
5:8=\dfrac{5}{8}

Read aloud: “2 to 3” = “2 divided by 3”.

2. Same kind only

Ratios need matching units — boys to girls, red paint to blue paint, cm to cm.

Rate mixes different units. Ratio stays same-to-same — the units cancel and leave a pure number:

Ratio ✓ same unit
\dfrac{12\,\cancel{\mathrm{cm}}}{5\,\cancel{\mathrm{cm}}}=\dfrac{12}{5}

Units cancel → no unit left.

Rate · different units
\dfrac{60\,\mathrm{km}}{2\,\mathrm{hr}}=30\,\mathrm{km/hr}

Units stay → this is a rate.

3. Equivalent ratios

Multiply or divide both parts by the same non-zero number:

2:3=4:6=6:9=\dfrac{2}{3}

Always simplify when you can — F2 papers love lowest terms.

4. 外項之積 = 內項之積

兩個比例相等：外項 × 外項 = 內項 × 內項

If a:b=c:d, then a,d are the extremes (外項) and b,c are the means (內項):

a\cdot d=b\cdot c

Proof

a:b=c:d

\Rightarrow\ \dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}

\Rightarrow\ \dfrac{a}{b}\cdot(bd)=\dfrac{c}{d}\cdot(bd)

(multiply both sides by bd)

\Rightarrow\ \dfrac{a}{\cancel{b}}\cdot(\cancel{b}\,d)=\dfrac{c}{\cancel{d}}\cdot(b\,\cancel{d})

\Rightarrow\ a\cdot d=b\cdot c

5. Numbers flip

From a coefficient equation, the ratio flips the numbers:

3a=2b\quad\Rightarrow\quad a:b=2:3

Proof

3a=2b

\Rightarrow\ \dfrac{3a}{3b}=\dfrac{2b}{3b}

(divide both sides by 3b)

\Rightarrow\ \dfrac{\cancel{3}a}{\cancel{3}b}=\dfrac{2\cancel{b}}{3\cancel{b}}

\Rightarrow\ \dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{2}{3}

\Rightarrow\ a:b=2:3

Memory trick: coefficients swap places into the ratio.

6. Part–whole

If red : blue = 2:3, first find total parts:

2+3=5\quad\text{(total parts)}
5 equal parts of the mix
1 2 3 4 5
red = 2 parts blue = 3 parts whole = 5
\text{red}=\dfrac{2}{5},\quad\text{blue}=\dfrac{3}{5}\quad\text{of the mix}

Example: mix 10\,\mathrm{L} → red gets \dfrac{2}{5}\times 10=4\,\mathrm{L}, blue gets 6\,\mathrm{L}.

Share / mixture questions always start from “total parts”.

Joining ratios · a:b:c

Given two ratios that share a letter, you cannot just stick the numbers together.

Example:

a : b = 2 : 5 b : c = 3 : 7
Common mistakes — do not write: a : b : c ≠ 2 : 5 : 7 a : b : c ≠ 2 : 3 : 7

Why? The middle letter b is 5 in the first ratio but 3 in the second — different “unit size”. Match b with the LCM first:

最小公倍數 · LCM
LCM of 5 and 3 = 15
a:b=2:5 × 3 → a : b = 6 : 15
b:c=3:7 × 5 → b : c = 15 : 35

Now both ratios use the same b = 15, so join them:

a:b:c=6:15:35
