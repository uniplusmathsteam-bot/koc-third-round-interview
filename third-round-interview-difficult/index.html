Part one · 02 · The plan

Ten minutes on paper first.

Before you open Cursor, fill in the one-page sheet in your folder. It is three questions long. We read it before we look at your tool, and it is worth marks on its own — a clear idea badly built scores better here than a pretty page that teaches nothing.

The three questions on the sheet

Two or three sentences each. Write in English or Chinese, whichever is faster for you.

  1. 1 · The idea of the toolIn plain words: what does the student see, and what do they actually do? “The student drags…”, “the student picks…”, “then the tool…”.
  2. 2 · The result you wantFinish this sentence: after using this tool, a student can ______ . One specific thing about special lines or centres — not “understand centres better”.
  3. 3 · Why it beats pen and paperName the thing your tool does that a teacher with a whiteboard cannot do as well — and say why that matters for this particular topic.

◈ Question 3 is the one we care most about If the honest answer is “it is the same as paper, but on a screen”, then the idea is not yet an interactive tool. Change the idea, not the wording.

Filling it in

One page, three boxes, nothing else to prepare.

An interactive tool for the four centres.

Build a browser tool that teaches one idea from JM28 — naming the four special lines, the centre each one produces, and a property of that centre (where it sits; the 2:1 median; equidistant from vertices or sides; or whether three lengths can form a triangle). The student must be able to change something, commit to an answer, and be told whether it is right and why.

Learning goal

After using your tool, a student should be able to look at a construction in a triangle, name the line and the centre — altitude → orthocentre; median → centroid; angle bisector → in-centre; perpendicular bisector → circumcentre — and say one true thing about that centre, and be corrected by the tool when they mix any part up.

◈ The topic is set, the teaching is yours JM28 is fixed. Whether you teach the four pairings, where H and O sit when the triangle becomes obtuse, or the triangle-inequality questions in the QUE set is your call.

Build one of these

  • SuggestedWhich line, and which centre? A triangle appears with one construction marked. The student names the line, names the centre, then predicts inside / on / outside — and only then checks.
  • AlternativeDrag it obtuse. The student drags a vertex from acute through right to obtuse and watches H and O move. They predict where each centre will sit before the tool draws it — so “always inside” becomes something they can break themselves.
  • AlternativeThree lengths. The triangle-inequality questions, movable: three sliders for the sides. The student predicts “can form / cannot form”, then the tool writes a + b ? c for the two shorter sides.

Your tool must include

  • At least two things the student can change, not just a Next button.
  • A point where the student commits to an answer before seeing the result.
  • Feedback that says right or wrong and explains why, using the wording written in the QUE set.
  • At least one case that is not the “always inside / always yes” answer — an obtuse triangle, or three lengths that cannot form a triangle.
  • A “Show worked example” button and a “Try another” button with a fresh figure.
  • One HTML file, no external libraries, readable on a laptop screen.

△

Getting the mathematics right

This is where the difficult version is actually difficult. AI is confident about the four centres and still swaps the pairings, or parks H inside every triangle.

01Check the four pairings: altitude → H, median → G, angle bisector → I, perpendicular bisector → O.
02Check a right triangle: H sits at the right-angle vertex, and O is the mid-point of the hypotenuse.
03Check an obtuse triangle: both H and O sit outside. G and I stay inside every triangle.
04Check the 2:1 median, and the triangle inequality: the two shorter sides must add to more than the longest.
05Work one QUE question by hand. If a figure is wrong and you cannot fix it in time, say so in the demonstration — we mark honesty higher than a hidden error.

06 · What matters

Show judgement, not perfection.

We are looking for a future KOC: someone who can turn a page of questions into teaching material a student will actually use, and explain the choices behind it — even when nobody printed the answers.

What we are not marking

Three things candidates worry about that carry no marks at all.

  • Not markedWhether you can write code yourself. You are expected to direct the AI, not to type JavaScript.
  • Not markedPolish and animation flourish. A plain tool that teaches beats a beautiful one that parks H in the wrong place.
  • Not markedHow many features you fit in. One idea done properly is the whole point of this round.

◈ Unfinished is acceptable Bring an incomplete tool and explain what was left and why. A candidate who knows what is wrong with their work scores better than one who does not notice.
